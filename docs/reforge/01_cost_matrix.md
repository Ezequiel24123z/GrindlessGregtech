## Method and settings

- Dump: `v0.17.0-max` (80,447 recipes; 66,859 used after the exclusions below). The cost of a key item is the base resources of the cheapest recipe chain, expanded through every intermediate.
- **Score** = base items + fluid litres, 1 per item and 1 per 1000 mB. Free resources (fluid:minecraft:water, fluid:gtceu:steam, fluid:gtceu:air, fluid:gtceu:nether_air, fluid:gtceu:ender_air, fluid:*sea_water) are tracked but score 0; machines (capital), tools and non-consumed inputs cost nothing, programmed circuits are free. EU is tracked (column `eu`) but not part of the score or of the recipe choice.
- **Base resources**: outputs of `gtceu:vein_drilling` (vein-drill ores and stone), raw ores (`*:raw_*` except `*rubber*`), natural fluids (every fluid no recipe makes, except 133 molten metals that count as their ingot at 144 mB per item), world items no recipe makes (minecraft:*, *:*_ore, *:*_leaves, gcyr:*moon*, gcyr:*regolith*, gcyr:prb_*), and the outputs of recipes that consume nothing (gas collectors and similar sources).
- **Multi-output recipes**: `full` (every output bears the whole recipe cost, so nothing is cheap just because it is a byproduct; outputs that are inert byproducts are never made for their own sake: fluid:minecraft:water, minecraft:cobblestone, minecraft:netherrack, minecraft:cobbled_deepslate, minecraft:end_stone, minecraft:dirt, minecraft:gravel, minecraft:sand). **Chanced outputs**: `expected` (count x chance). **Catalysts**: expected consumption. **Tag inputs**: the cheapest member. **Loops**: items are priced in increasing cost and each recipe fires once, so a loop that multiplies matter (dust packing and unpacking) cannot pump a cost down.
- **Recipe choice**: the cheapest chain by score, ties by dump order. Excluded: types `gtceu:vein_drilling`, categories `*recycling*, *decomposition*`, ids `*/uncraft*, *_recycling*, */unpack*, *decompress*`.
- Solver converged after 28,558 relaxations.

## Reading the baseline

Measured on the v0.17.0-max dump. Index = geometric mean of the median score of the nine core components (hull, motor, pump, piston, conveyor, robot arm, emitter, sensor, field generator).

| Era | LV | MV | HV | EV | IV | LuV | ZPM | UV | UHV | UEV | UIV | UXV | OPV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Core index | 0.75 | 1.85 | 1.16 | 2.48 | 2.45 | 18.9 | 102 | 250 | 691 | 2,050 | 3,690 | 11,300 | 944 |
| x previous | - | 2.5 | 0.6 | 2.1 | 1.0 | 7.7 | 5.4 | 2.5 | 2.8 | 3.0 | 1.8 | 3.1 | 0.08 |

- **LV to IV is nearly flat** (0.75 to 2.45, x3.3 over four eras). HV and IV are cheaper than the era before for several components. This is the opposite of "quantity must be high and expensive".
- **Cliffs.** LuV x7.7 (emitter x19, sensor x23, conveyor x12, robot arm x11), ZPM x5.4 (emitter and sensor x35 to x37, field generator x33, the `assembly_hall` capstone x169), UEV (hull x360, energy hatches x378 to x465 against UHV).
- **Regressions.** OPV components cost 2% to 11% of their UXV equivalents (hull excepted). The UV and UXV capstones are cheaper than the previous era's.
- **Gaps.** 75 key items have no price: the OPV and MAX chains are blocked by the tungsten / `field_can` loop (the only tungsten source is the loop itself) and by items with no recipe (`gtnn:*` late circuits, `r_process_decompressor`). These need fixing before any OPV or MAX number means anything.
- **Superconductor wire** is not a reliable series: it is priced per wire from cheap dusts and falls to 0.00 at UIV. Treat it as a flag, not as an input to the curve.

Caveat on absolute values: the solver takes the cheapest chain, so in-pack arbitrage (alloy decomposition, ash separation, byproduct loops) pushes base-metal costs low. Absolute scores are a floor; era-to-era ratios are the dependable part.

## Proposed curve

This is a proposal. The orchestrator records the accepted curve in DECISIONS.md. Phase 2 applies it from data and tables, never by hand-editing recipes.

**Rule.** Target core index T(era) = 3 x 2.5^n, with n = 0 at MV. Each era costs 2.5x the previous one in base items plus fluid litres, after expanding every intermediate. Time (`duration`) stays as it is; only quantities and ingredient counts move.

| Era | LV (review) | MV | HV | EV | IV | LuV | ZPM | UV | UHV | UEV | UIV | UXV | OPV | MAX |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Target index | 0.75, floor only | 3 | 8 | 19 | 47 | 117 | 293 | 732 | 1,831 | 4,578 | 11,444 | 28,610 | 71,526 | 178,814 |
| Multiplier vs baseline | x1 | x1.6 | x6.5 | x7.6 | x19 | x6.2 | x2.9 | x2.9 | x2.7 | x2.2 | x3.1 | x2.5 | x76 | n/a |

One line: **cumulative cost x2.5 per era from MV (target 3 at MV, 47 at IV, 732 at UV, 28,610 at UXV, 178,814 at MAX); needed multiplier vs the baseline x1.6 / 6.5 / 7.6 / 19 / 6.2 / 2.9 / 2.9 / 2.7 / 2.2 / 3.1 / 2.5 / 76 / n/a for MV to MAX.**

**Reasoning.**
- A single geometric rate is steeper than the baseline up to IV (x3.3 over four eras, against x2.5 per era proposed) and flatter above ZPM, where the baseline already compounds at x2.5 to x3. Every era becomes clearly more expensive than the last, which is the owner decision ("MV to MAX: full rebalance, scaling steeply per era").
- The multiplier is a ratio of targets, not a constant. It removes the measured cliffs (LuV, ZPM, UEV) and regressions (HV, IV, OPV) in the same pass, so Phase 2 only needs one table of per-era factors. This also fixes the era shape complexity.md assumes: multiblock counts grow ~x1.35 per era, cost grows x2.5, so each multiblock is also a bigger material commitment.
- The multiplier on the cumulative target is not the multiplier to apply to recipes. A recipe of era E carries the already-scaled cost of its lower-era components. Phase 2 should therefore solve the direct-input factor per era in order (MV first), re-run this tool with `--compare 01_cost_matrix.csv`, and iterate until each era's index is within about 10% of the target. Expect direct factors below the cumulative ones.
- Final items and key recipes first: hull, energy hatch, circuits, the nine core components, controllers and capstones carry the curve. Intermediates change only as the solver requires.
- LV: review only. Fix the LV items that cost under about 0.2 (casings, wires, coils, hulls, buses; the 12 listed) and the ulv-to-lv regressions (hull, energy hatch), with no uniform multiplier.
- Quantity, not time: raise item counts and fluid volumes; keep `duration` and EU/t.
- MAX+ ("far beyond MAX"): same rule with n continuing, 2.5x per era would only give about 446,000 at the first MAX+ era. The owner asked for far beyond, so use a steeper rate after MAX (suggest x5 per era) and keep it as a separate table row so it can change independently.

**Risks.**
- **Tank and slot capacity.** The largest factors (x6.5 to x19, HV to IV) land on machines with the smallest hatches. Any recipe whose fluid input times the factor exceeds the input hatch or tank of its machine tier, or whose distinct item inputs exceed the machine's slots, cannot run at all. Mitigation, in order: add ingredients and intermediate steps (complexity first), use higher-tier machines or multiblock buses, and only then raise a count. The parallel slot and tank capacity linter should receive the per-era factors from this table and fail Phase 2 outputs that exceed it. Single-item inputs are further capped at a stack of 64 per slot.
- **Automation throughput.** Every input must stay automatable. 2.5x quantities per era with unchanged recipe time raise belt and pipe throughput requirements by the same factor; check that the first Phase 2 tier (HV to IV) stays within pipe and bus flow limits.
- **Arbitrage.** If a cheap decomposition or loop stays, scaling its inputs does not raise the true cost. Re-run the matrix after Phase 2 and look at the top-contributor table, not only the score.
- **Unreachable items.** OPV and MAX targets cannot be validated until the `field_can` tungsten loop and the recipe gaps are fixed; the x76 OPV factor is an upper bound that assumes UXV is the right predecessor.
- **Bands.** The thresholds in `tools/analysis/cost_matrix.json` (ratio bands, LV threshold, dominance) are initial values chosen from this dump. They are settings, not facts.
- **Saves.** Changing quantities never removes items; it does change what existing machines consume. Flag in the release notes.
