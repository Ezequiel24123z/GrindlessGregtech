## Method and settings

- Dump: `v0.17.0-max` (80,447 recipes; 66,859 used after the exclusions below). The cost of a key item is the base resources of the cheapest recipe chain, expanded through every intermediate.
- **Score** = base items + fluid litres, 1 per item and 1 per 1000 mB. Free resources (fluid:minecraft:water, fluid:gtceu:steam, fluid:gtceu:air, fluid:gtceu:nether_air, fluid:gtceu:ender_air, fluid:*sea_water) are tracked but score 0; machines (capital), tools and non-consumed inputs cost nothing, programmed circuits are free. EU is tracked (column `eu`) but not part of the score or of the recipe choice.
- **Base resources**: outputs of `gtceu:vein_drilling` (vein-drill ores and stone), raw ores (`*:raw_*` except `*rubber*`), natural fluids (every fluid no recipe makes, except 133 molten metals that count as their ingot at 144 mB per item), world items no recipe makes (minecraft:*, *:*_ore, *:*_leaves, gcyr:*moon*, gcyr:*regolith*, gcyr:prb_*), and the outputs of recipes that consume nothing (gas collectors and similar sources).
- **Multi-output recipes**: `full` (every output bears the whole recipe cost, so nothing is cheap just because it is a byproduct; outputs that are inert byproducts are never made for their own sake: fluid:minecraft:water, minecraft:cobblestone, minecraft:netherrack, minecraft:cobbled_deepslate, minecraft:end_stone, minecraft:dirt, minecraft:gravel, minecraft:sand). **Chanced outputs**: `expected` (count x chance). **Catalysts**: expected consumption. **Tag inputs**: the cheapest member. **Loops**: items are priced in increasing cost and each recipe fires once, so a loop that multiplies matter (dust packing and unpacking) cannot pump a cost down.
- **Recipe choice**: the cheapest acyclic chain by score, ties by dump order. Items are first priced in increasing cost (Knuth), then a label-correcting pass lets a cheaper route through a dearer intermediate replace an item's choice (per-unit division, e.g. 1 plate -> 4 foil, is not monotonic), but only when the item is not already in the derivation of the candidate's inputs. Excluded: types `gtceu:vein_drilling`, categories `*recycling*, *decomposition*`, ids `*/uncraft*, *_recycling*, */unpack*, *decompress*`.
- **Self-check**: non-loop improvements left: 0 (205,286 applied in 438,160 recipe evaluations; 28,558 items priced by the first pass). Fixed point reached.

## Reading the baseline

Measured on the v0.17.0-max dump with the cheapest acyclic chain (see Method: Knuth pass plus label-correcting pass, 0 non-loop improvements left). Index = geometric mean of the median score of the nine core components (hull, motor, pump, piston, conveyor, robot arm, emitter, sensor, field generator).

| Era | LV | MV | HV | EV | IV | LuV | ZPM | UV | UHV | UEV | UIV | UXV | OPV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Core index | 0.19 | 0.51 | 0.56 | 0.82 | 0.91 | 7.88 | 36.1 | 168 | 519 | 1,419 | 2,594 | 7,937 | 242 |
| x previous | - | 2.7 | 1.1 | 1.5 | 1.1 | 8.6 | 4.6 | 4.7 | 3.1 | 2.7 | 1.8 | 3.1 | 0.03 |

- **LV to IV is nearly flat** (0.19 to 0.91, about x1.5 per era; HV +10%, IV +11%). Several IV components are cheaper than at EV (motor x0.79, piston x0.71) and HV field generator is x0.37 of MV. This is the opposite of "quantity must be high and expensive".
- **Cliffs.** LuV x8.6 (motor x11, conveyor x14, emitter x16, sensor x17), ZPM x4.6 (emitter and sensor x43, field generator x39, hull x13, the `assembly_hall` capstone x215), UV (motor, pump, piston, conveyor x20 to x36), UEV (hull x339, energy hatches x486 to x726 against UHV).
- **Regressions.** OPV components cost 1% to 4% of their UXV equivalents (hull excepted) and the OPV circuit is x0.01 of UXV. The UV, UEV and UXV capstones are cheaper than the previous era's (x0.37, x0.67, x0.75), and the UEV controller median is x0.40 of UHV.
- **Gaps.** 75 key items have no price: the OPV and MAX chains are blocked by the tungsten / `field_can` loop (the only tungsten source is the loop itself) and by items with no recipe (`gtnn:*` late circuits, `r_process_decompressor`). These need fixing before any OPV or MAX number means anything.
- **Superconductor wire** is not a reliable series: it is priced per wire from cheap dusts and falls to 0.00 at UIV. Treat it as a flag, not as an input to the curve.

Effect of the solver repair (RF-1B repair 1): the previous solver finalized an item when first popped, so per-unit division (1 plate -> 4 foil, bulk component-assembly recipes) hid cheaper routes. The core index fell for every era (before -> after: LV 0.75 -> 0.19, MV 1.85 -> 0.51, HV 1.16 -> 0.56, EV 2.48 -> 0.82, IV 2.45 -> 0.91, LuV 18.9 -> 7.9, ZPM 102 -> 36, UV 250 -> 168, UHV 691 -> 519, UEV 2,050 -> 1,419, UIV 3,690 -> 2,594, UXV 11,300 -> 7,937, OPV 944 -> 242). The drop is larger in the early eras, so the era ratios changed (ZPM to UV x2.5 became x4.7; LV to IV is flatter).

Caveat on absolute values: the score is the cost of the cheapest **acyclic** chain, not of the intended route. A chain that multiplies matter without closing a loop is used whenever it wins: ash separation, alloy decomposition and tiny or small dust splitting (for example a steel plate traces to about 0.004 base items through `ash_separation` and tiny-dust splitting), and one raw ore (`netherrack_blue_topaz_ore`) is 53% to 65% of every ZPM and later capstone. Absolute scores are therefore a lower bound on the intended cost and can be far below it; they are only as meaningful as the recipe set is free of such amplifiers. Era-to-era ratios remain a fair measure of the cheapest available route. Before Phase 2 calibrates absolute numbers, the matter-multiplying recipes that the top-contributor table and `--trace` expose should be reviewed and, where they are bugs, excluded through `excludeRecipes` in `cost_matrix.json` (a setting, not a code change).

## Proposed curve

This is a proposal. The orchestrator records the accepted curve in DECISIONS.md. Phase 2 applies it from data and tables, never by hand-editing recipes.

**Rule (unchanged from the first proposal).** Target core index T(era) = 3 x 2.5^n, with n = 0 at MV. Each era costs 2.5x the previous one in base items plus fluid litres, after expanding every intermediate. Time (`duration`) stays as it is; only quantities and ingredient counts move. The repaired data does not argue against the rule: from IV to UXV the baseline already compounds at about x3.1 per era, so the rule never asks a late era to get cheaper (the smallest multiplier is x3.2, at UEV). What the data changes is the size of the early factors, because LV to IV is flatter than first measured.

| Era | LV (review) | MV | HV | EV | IV | LuV | ZPM | UV | UHV | UEV | UIV | UXV | OPV | MAX |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline index | 0.19 | 0.51 | 0.56 | 0.82 | 0.91 | 7.88 | 36.1 | 168 | 519 | 1,419 | 2,594 | 7,937 | 242 | n/a |
| Target index | 0.19, floor only | 3 | 7.5 | 19 | 47 | 117 | 293 | 732 | 1,831 | 4,578 | 11,444 | 28,610 | 71,526 | 178,814 |
| Multiplier vs baseline | x1 | x5.9 | x13 | x23 | x51 | x15 | x8.1 | x4.4 | x3.5 | x3.2 | x4.4 | x3.6 | x295 | n/a |

One line: **cumulative cost x2.5 per era from MV (target 3 at MV, 47 at IV, 732 at UV, 28,610 at UXV, 178,814 at MAX); needed multiplier vs the baseline x5.9 / 13 / 23 / 51 / 15 / 8.1 / 4.4 / 3.5 / 3.2 / 4.4 / 3.6 / 295 / n/a for MV to MAX.**

**Reasoning.**
- A single geometric rate is steeper than the baseline up to IV (x4.9 over four eras, against x2.5 per era proposed) and slightly flatter than the late baseline (about x3.1 per era from IV to UXV). Every era becomes clearly more expensive than the last, which is the owner decision ("MV to MAX: full rebalance, scaling steeply per era").
- The multiplier is a ratio of targets, not a constant. It removes the measured cliffs (LuV, ZPM, UV, UEV) and regressions (HV, IV, OPV) in the same pass, so Phase 2 only needs one table of per-era factors. This also fixes the era shape complexity.md assumes: multiblock counts grow ~x1.35 per era, cost grows x2.5, so each multiblock is also a bigger material commitment.
- The anchor (3 at MV) is a choice, not a measurement. It keeps every multiplier at x3.2 or more. If the capacity linter finds the HV to IV factors (x13 to x51) unreachable, lower the anchor to 2 (MV x3.9, IV x34, UEV x2.1) rather than the rate; both stay at 2 or above in every era. This is an alternative, not the proposal.
- The multiplier on the cumulative target is not the multiplier to apply to recipes. A recipe of era E carries the already-scaled cost of its lower-era components. Phase 2 should therefore solve the direct-input factor per era in order (MV first), re-run this tool with `--compare 01_cost_matrix.csv`, and iterate until each era's index is within about 10% of the target. Expect direct factors below the cumulative ones.
- Final items and key recipes first: hull, energy hatch, circuits, the nine core components, controllers and capstones carry the curve. Intermediates change only as the solver requires.
- LV: review only. Fix the LV items that cost under about 0.1 (casings, coils, wires, hulls, buses; the 12 listed, three of them at 0.00) and the ulv-to-lv regressions (hull x0.63, energy hatch x0.23), with no uniform multiplier.
- Quantity, not time: raise item counts and fluid volumes; keep `duration` and EU/t.
- MAX+ ("far beyond MAX"): same rule with n continuing, 2.5x per era would only give about 446,000 at the first MAX+ era. The owner asked for far beyond, so use a steeper rate after MAX (suggest x5 per era) and keep it as a separate table row so it can change independently.

**Risks.**
- **Tank and slot capacity.** The largest factors (x13 to x51, HV to IV) land on machines with the smallest hatches. Any recipe whose fluid input times the factor exceeds the input hatch or tank of its machine tier, or whose distinct item inputs exceed the machine's slots, cannot run at all. Mitigation, in order: add ingredients and intermediate steps (complexity first), use higher-tier machines or multiblock buses, and only then raise a count. The parallel slot and tank capacity linter should receive the per-era factors from this table and fail Phase 2 outputs that exceed it. Single-item inputs are further capped at a stack of 64 per slot.
- **Automation throughput.** Every input must stay automatable. 2.5x quantities per era with unchanged recipe time raise belt and pipe throughput requirements by the same factor; check that the first Phase 2 tier (HV to IV) stays within pipe and bus flow limits.
- **Arbitrage.** The matrix prices the cheapest acyclic chain, and matter-multiplying chains (ash separation, decomposition, dust splitting) dominate some items. Scaling an input that such a chain bypasses does not raise the cost. Re-run the matrix after Phase 2 and look at the top-contributor table, not only the score; remove genuine amplifiers via `excludeRecipes` first.
- **Unreachable items.** OPV and MAX targets cannot be validated until the `field_can` tungsten loop and the recipe gaps are fixed; the x295 OPV factor is an upper bound that assumes UXV is the right predecessor (the OPV baseline is itself a regression).
- **Bands.** The thresholds in `tools/analysis/cost_matrix.json` (ratio bands, LV threshold, dominance) are initial values chosen from this dump. They are settings, not facts.
- **Saves.** Changing quantities never removes items; it does change what existing machines consume. Flag in the release notes.
